The fb_sign invariant

fbSign names the gentle flank. The spectral purity curve is asymmetric: the slower falls off sharply on the low-current side and gently on the high side; the xbeam is the reverse. getSlope walks from the peak in the fbSign direction, so fb.m is always measured on the gentle flank — negative for the slower, positive for the xbeam.

Everything directional follows from that one fact. relock extrapolates along the gentle slope, so it can only travel in the −fbSign direction, and therefore can only approach the peak from the fbSign side. Every path that hands off to relock must leave the channel on that side. That constraint fixes the recovery start offset, the recovery sweep direction, the lost-state bump, the overshoot retreat, and the lost jump-back.

All of those collapse to the single test dx * fbSign < 0 and the single direction −fbSign. Note that bumpUp deliberately does not consult fbSign: in normal operation both channels need the current bumped up, and the probe determines direction empirically.

The role of goodCode

goodCode is the only persistent memory of where the lock actually was. It is written on every success — in bumpUp, relock and recover — and read back in three places:

  • Recovery starts its sweep at goodCode + fbSign·(n+1)·fwhm, so a stale value sends the sweep to the wrong region entirely.
  • A recovery sweep that reaches the rail without finding the peak parks the DAC back at goodCode, rather than abandoning the laser at zero or full scale.
  • The lost branch of bumpUp jumps to goodCode + fbSign·fwhm.
Historical note. goodCode was previously overwritten after every SPC run with Slope.x0, which was a sweep-relative step offset rather than an absolute DAC code. It therefore landed near the bottom of the DAC range, and recovery swept away from the peak from a nonsensical starting point. Recovery almost certainly never succeeded before this was fixed.

The two handoffs

Both exits at the bottom of the staircase must leave the channel on the gentle flank, because that is the only side relock can work from.

lost:       setDac(ch, ch.goodCode + ch.fbSign * ch.fwhm);

overshoot:  if (effective_sign != ch.fbSign)
              setDac(ch, ch.dacCode - effective_sign * 0.25 * ch.fwhm);

The retreat is skipped when effective_sign == fbSign, because in that case overshooting already left the channel on the gentle side — retreating would push it back across onto the sharp side.

Known weaknesses

The retreat fraction is not self-consistent. The 0.25·FWHM retreat reliably crosses the peak only because the overshoot is at most one 18 LSB step while the retreat is 87 LSB. That relationship depends on bump_thresh staying near 0.05. At 0.25 the step would itself be 87 LSB and the retreat would no longer be guaranteed to cross. Expressing the retreat as a multiple of the step rather than of the FWHM would close this.

Recovery blocks the main loop. A full failed sweep holds the loop for around 13 s, during which no commands are serviced and no traces are sent. The host cannot distinguish this from a hang.

Peak height is a weak discriminant. It responds to FP alignment, photodiode gain and master power as much as to injection current, and at the top of the plateau it carries no sign information at all — which is the reason this search machinery has to exist. A beatnote detector or a dither-and-demodulate scheme would supply a true bipolar error signal and allow most of this state machine to be replaced by an integrator.

The lost branch of bumpUp is newly reachable. It was written, revised and carried across several firmware versions without ever executing. Treat it as new code rather than as restored behaviour.
